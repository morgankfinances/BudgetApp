Getting started

What is Coinrose?

Coinrose is a budgeting app for households. You upload the statements you download from your bank or card company, and Coinrose combines them into one place: categorized spending, budgets, reports, and insights, shared with everyone in your household.

It never connects to your bank. You decide exactly what goes in.

How do I start?
  1. Create an account and a household (or join someone else's with their invite code).
  2. Download a statement from your bank's website and upload it on the Upload page.
  3. Categorize your transactions. Coinrose learns from your choices and fills in categories it recognizes.
  4. Set budgets on Planning, then follow along on Budget and the Overview.

The guided tour walks through each page. You can replay it anytime from Settings → View tutorial.

Why doesn't Coinrose connect to my bank automatically?

On purpose. Automatic bank connections mean handing your bank login, or a standing link to your accounts, to a third-party data company. Uploading statements keeps you in control: Coinrose only ever has the transactions you choose to give it, and nothing else about your bank accounts. The trade-off is a few minutes of downloading and uploading now and then.

Uploading statements

What file types can I upload?

CSV, Excel (.xlsx or .xls), and OFX or QFX (sometimes called "Quicken" or "Money" format).

If your bank offers OFX or QFX, use it. Those files include the bank's own ID for every transaction, so Coinrose can tell with certainty whether a transaction is new or already uploaded.

Where do I find the download on my bank's website?

It's usually on the account's transaction history page, under a button like Download, Export, or a download icon. Choose a date range, then a format (CSV, Excel, OFX, QFX, or "Quicken"). Some banks only offer it on their website, not in their phone app.

The columns came in wrong. How do I fix that?

After choosing a file, Coinrose shows which column it's using for the date, description, and amounts. Change any that are wrong before continuing. Coinrose remembers your choices for that account, so the next upload from the same bank fills them in automatically. You can also change them later from Accounts → Settings on that account.

What happens if I upload the same transactions twice?

By default, Coinrose sets aside exact matches (same account, date, amount, and description, or the same bank ID) instead of importing them again, so overlapping statements are fine. Anything set aside is listed under Skipped duplicates on the Transactions page, where you can restore it if it was actually a separate purchase.

You can change this in Settings → Importing: skip duplicates (the default), import everything and flag likely duplicates, or turn duplicate checks off.

Categories, splits, and comments

Why are some categories marked "Suggested"?

Coinrose fills in categories it recognizes from how you've categorized similar transactions before. They count right away, and stay marked Suggested until you confirm or change them. Coinrose only learns from categories you've confirmed or chosen yourself. You can turn this off in Settings → Importing.

How do I split a purchase between categories?

On the Transactions page, choose Split on the transaction. Add a line for each category with its amount, or use the quick options to split evenly or by percentage. The lines have to add up to the transaction's total. Budgets and reports then count each piece in its own category.

What are comments for?

Notes on a transaction that everyone in your household can see, like "Is this the vet bill?" Use the 💬 button on a transaction. Searching on the Transactions page also searches comments.

What does "excluded" mean for a category?

Excluded categories don't count as spending or income in budgets and reports. They're meant for money that isn't really either, like moving money between your own accounts.

Transfers between accounts

Why does Coinrose ask about "possible transfers"?

When money moves between two of your own accounts (paying the credit card from checking, or moving money to savings), it shows up twice: once leaving one account and once arriving in the other. Neither is really spending or income. Coinrose looks for matching amounts in two of your accounts within a few days of each other and asks you to confirm. Confirmed pairs are marked as transfers and left out of spending, so nothing is counted twice.

If a suggestion is wrong, choose Not a transfer and it won't be suggested again.

Budgets

What's the difference between "Spend" and "Accumulate" budgets?

A Spend budget is a limit that resets each period, like $600 a month for groceries. An Accumulate budget adds its amount every period and carries over what you don't use, which suits saving toward something, like car repairs or a vacation.

Can I budget weekly instead of monthly?

Yes. Each budget can be weekly or monthly. Separately, Reports can follow your pay schedule: weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, or monthly.

Budget Groups let several categories share one budget, like Groceries and Dining Out under a single Food limit.

Can I import a budget from YNAB, or share mine?

Yes. At the bottom of Planning, choose Import a budget file. Coinrose shows exactly what would change before anything does, and you can undo it from Data History for 7 days.

  • From YNAB: in YNAB's web app, click your plan's name, choose Export Plan, unzip it, and import the plan file (not the register). Coinrose uses what you assigned each category, averaged over the last 3 months or from the most recent month.
  • To share: choose Download my budget as a template and send the file. It holds budgets only, with no transactions or spending history, and leaves out your income unless you include it.

A template is a CSV file you can also make in any spreadsheet, with the columns Kind (Income, Category, or Group), Name, Amount, Period (monthly or weekly), Type (Spend or Accumulate), Goal, and Group. For example:

Kind,Name,Amount,Period,Type,Goal,Group
Category,Groceries,600,monthly,Spend,,Food
Category,Car Repairs,75,monthly,Accumulate,1500,
Group,Food,750,monthly,Spend,,

The Planning page can also download a full example to start from.

Insights and recurring bills

How does Coinrose find recurring bills?

It looks for charges from the same place on a steady schedule: weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, monthly, every three months, or yearly. It needs at least three charges (two for quarterly or yearly ones). Amounts can vary for bills like utilities, but regular shopping at the same store isn't counted as a bill.

If something is listed that isn't really recurring, choose Not recurring to hide it. You can bring it back later.

Insights says something odd. Why?

Insights only know what's been uploaded. A missing statement, an uncategorized transaction, or an unconfirmed transfer can all skew them. Comparisons ignore a partial first month and only average months that actually had spending in a category, but they work best with a few complete months of records.

Account balances

How does Coinrose know my account balance?

It doesn't know it for certain: it estimates it. On the Accounts page, choose Track this account's balance and enter a balance from a statement or your bank's website, plus the day it was for. Coinrose then works out the balance on other days from the transactions you've uploaded, both after that day and before it.

Because Coinrose isn't connected to your bank, the estimate can differ from your real balance: for example, because of pending transactions, fees, or statements you haven't uploaded yet. If it drifts, enter a new starting balance from a recent statement.

How do I track a credit card or loan?

When entering its starting balance, tick This is a credit card or loan and enter the amount you owe. Purchases then increase what's owed and payments reduce it, the way your statement reads.

Why does the balance chart stop at "Earliest uploaded data"?

That's the day before your oldest uploaded transaction for that account. Your real account goes back further, but Coinrose can't know its balance before the records you've uploaded.

Households and members

How do I invite someone?

Open Settings and share your household's invite code. They enter it when they sign up, or later from Settings, which sends a request that an owner approves. Codes expire after 7 days, and owners can make a new one anytime.

What's the difference between an owner and a member?

Both can see and change the household's finances. Owners can also approve join requests, make new invite codes, remove members, and change roles. A household can have more than one owner.

Can I belong to more than one household?

Not at the same time: each person belongs to one household. To move to a different one, use Settings → Join a different household and enter its invite code. You'll keep using your current household normally until someone in the other household approves the request. Each household's data is completely separate.

Backups and undo

How do I back up my data?

On the Backup page, choose Download complete backup. It's one file with everything: accounts and balances, every transaction with its splits, transfers, and comments, categories, budgets, and your household's settings. Keep it somewhere safe, since it contains your financial records.

The same page restores from a complete backup, showing what's in the file before replacing anything. The spreadsheet (CSV) copies there are for opening in Excel or Google Sheets; they don't include everything.

I made a mistake. Can I undo it?

Yes, for 7 days. Settings → Data History lists recent changes, and you can restore your household's data to how it was before any of them, including restores from a backup.

Signing in and two-step sign-in

Do I need a password?

No. You can sign in with a link sent to your email each time, or set a password in Settings if you'd rather type one.

How do I turn on two-step sign-in?

In Settings → Two-step sign-in, scan the QR code with an authenticator app (like Google Authenticator, 1Password, or Authy) and enter the 6-digit code it shows. After that, signing in takes your password or email link plus a code from the app.

You'll also get 10 one-time backup codes. Save them somewhere safe, like a password manager or a printed copy. They're shown only once.

I lost my phone. How do I get in?

Sign in as usual, then choose Lost your phone? Use a backup code and enter one of your backup codes. Each works once. Using one turns two-step sign-in off, so you can set it up again with your new phone (and get fresh backup codes).

No backup codes either? Email morgankfinances@gmail.com from the address you sign in with.

Privacy and your data

Does Coinrose sell my data or show ads?

No. Coinrose doesn't sell or share your data, and doesn't show ads. See the Privacy Policy for the details.

What does Coinrose store about me?

Your email address, your sign-in details, and what you put into Coinrose: transactions, categories, budgets, and settings. It never stores bank logins or full account numbers. The Privacy Policy lists everything.

How do I delete my account?

In Settings → Delete my account. Your sign-in is deleted, along with any household where you're the only member. Households you share with others keep their data for the remaining members. Download a complete backup first if you want a copy.

Troubleshooting

My sign-in email didn't arrive.
  • Check your spam or junk folder, and search for "Coinrose."
  • Wait a minute or two. Emails occasionally take a moment.
  • Sign-in emails are limited to a few per hour, so repeated requests can be held back. Try again a little later.
  • If it never arrives, email morgankfinances@gmail.com.
Amounts came in with the wrong sign (spending shows as income).

Banks show amounts differently: some use one column with negative numbers for spending, some use separate columns for money in and out. On the column step of an upload, check how the amount columns are set, and fix them for that account under Accounts → Settings. Then delete the affected transactions and upload the file again.

Something's not working right.

Reloading the page fixes most problems, and your saved data is always safe. If it keeps happening, email morgankfinances@gmail.com with what you were doing and what you saw. A screenshot helps a lot.
